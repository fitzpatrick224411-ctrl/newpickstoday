## What Makes a Good VPN for Travel?

A travel VPN has to do more than hide your IP address. It needs to work reliably on poor Wi-Fi, protect you on unfamiliar networks, help you access home-region content, and avoid draining your phone battery during long travel days.

The most important travel VPN features include:

- **Strong encryption:** Modern VPNs typically use AES-256 or ChaCha20 encryption depending on the protocol. This helps protect your traffic on public Wi-Fi networks such as airports, hotels, cafés, coworking spaces, and train stations.
- **Fast protocols:** WireGuard-based protocols are popular because they usually deliver good speeds and quick reconnections. Surfshark uses WireGuard and other protocols, including OpenVPN and IKEv2 depending on platform support.
- **Kill switch:** A kill switch helps block internet traffic if the VPN connection drops, reducing the chance of accidental exposure on untrusted networks.
- **Large server network:** More server locations give you better odds of finding a fast nearby connection or accessing content from your home country.
- **Streaming support:** Travelers often want access to their home Netflix, BBC iPlayer, Hulu, Disney+, YouTube TV, sports platforms, or local TV apps. VPNs can help, but streaming access is never guaranteed and can change frequently.
- **Unlimited or generous device coverage:** Travelers often carry a phone, laptop, tablet, smartwatch, work device, and streaming device. Unlimited connections are a major advantage.
- **Obfuscation or “camouflage” features:** These may help on restrictive networks that block VPN traffic, though they are not guaranteed to work everywhere.
- **No-logs policy and independent audits:** A strong privacy stance matters, especially when routing your internet traffic through a third party.

For travel, usability matters as much as privacy. If an app is confusing, slow to connect, or unreliable on mobile data and hotel Wi-Fi, you are less likely to use it when it matters.

## How a VPN Helps You Access Home Content Abroad

One of the most common reasons people install a VPN before traveling is to access content from home. Streaming platforms, sports services, news sites, and TV networks often license content by region. When you travel, your IP address tells websites and apps that you are in a different country, which can change what you can watch.

A VPN lets you connect through a server in your home country. For example, a traveler from the U.S. in Spain could connect to a U.S. VPN server, while a traveler from the UK in Thailand could connect to a UK server. This may restore access to familiar libraries, local news, or subscription services.

However, there are limits. Streaming providers actively block some VPN servers, and availability can change without warning. A server that works today may not work next month. VPNs also do not replace a valid subscription. You still need an account with the service you want to use, and you should follow the platform’s terms of service.

For the best results when streaming abroad:

1. Install and test the VPN before leaving home.
2. Connect to a server in your home country.
3. Clear app cache or browser cookies if the service still shows the wrong region.
4. Try a different server if playback is blocked.
5. Use the VPN provider’s live chat or help center for current working server recommendations.
6. Keep your streaming apps updated.
7. Avoid relying on a VPN for must-watch live sports unless you have tested it in advance.

Surfshark is generally a good streaming VPN, but again, no provider can guarantee access to every platform at all times.

## Working Remotely With a VPN While Traveling

Remote workers have different VPN needs from casual travelers. If you are handling client files, company email, financial accounts, admin dashboards, or confidential documents, a VPN can reduce risk on networks you do not control.

Public Wi-Fi is convenient but not always trustworthy. Attackers may set up fake hotspots with names similar to airports, hotels, or cafés. Poorly configured networks can expose local device names or allow certain types of snooping. A VPN encrypts your internet connection between your device and the VPN server, making it harder for people on the same network to inspect your traffic.

That said, a consumer VPN is not the same thing as a corporate security program. If your employer provides a company VPN, zero-trust access tool, endpoint detection software, or mobile device management system, use those as instructed. A personal VPN like Surfshark is best for general network protection and personal privacy; it may not satisfy your employer’s compliance requirements.

Travel workers should also consider:

- **Two-factor authentication:** Use authenticator apps or hardware security keys where possible.
- **Password manager:** Avoid reusing passwords across work and travel services.
- **Device encryption:** Enable full-disk encryption on laptops and strong passcodes on phones.
- **Backups:** Keep important files backed up before crossing borders.
- **Local laws:** VPN rules differ by country. In some places, VPN use is restricted or politically sensitive.
- **Split tunneling:** Surfshark’s Bypasser feature can route some apps outside the VPN if needed. This can help with banking apps or local services that dislike VPN connections.

A VPN is one layer of protection, not a complete travel security plan.
