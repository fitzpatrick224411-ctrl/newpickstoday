## What Is CyberGhost and Why Does It Still Matter?

The pitch is straightforward: a large global server network, beginner-friendly apps, dedicated streaming servers, and long-term pricing that looks very affordable at first glance. On paper, it checks almost every box. The question is whether the real-world experience holds up.

CyberGhost is particularly popular with two groups: people who want a no-fuss VPN they can set up in minutes, and streaming fans who want to unlock geo-restricted content. Inside the app, you can filter servers by the specific platform you want to unblock — Netflix US, BBC iPlayer, Disney+, Hulu, Amazon Prime Video, and others — and the app routes you directly to a server that's been verified to work with that service.

In practice, this approach works surprisingly well for most mainstream platforms. BBC iPlayer access from outside the UK also performs reliably on the dedicated UK servers.

Speed on those dedicated streaming servers is generally solid. CyberGhost runs servers in over 100 countries (verify current count at vendor site), giving it one of the wider geographic footprints in the industry.

One honest caveat: streaming server availability isn't static. VPN providers and streaming platforms are in an ongoing cat-and-mouse game. During peak periods or after a streaming platform update, you may temporarily hit a wall. That said, CyberGhost's dedicated labeling system means you're not left guessing — you know which servers are supposed to work, and their support team generally updates the list when changes occur.

## Privacy Policy and the Kape Technologies Question

Here's where we have to be fully transparent, because this is the most controversial aspect of CyberGhost and any honest review needs to address it directly.

CyberGhost maintains a strict no-logs policy, meaning it claims not to record your browsing activity, connection timestamps, IP addresses, or bandwidth usage in a way that could be tied back to you. The company is headquartered in Romania, which is generally considered a favorable jurisdiction — Romania is outside the Five Eyes and Fourteen Eyes intelligence-sharing alliances, and there is no mandatory data retention law requiring VPNs to log user activity.

CyberGhost also publishes a quarterly transparency report that details any data requests it receives from authorities and how it responds. This is a genuine plus: most VPNs don't publish this level of detail.

**The controversy:** CyberGhost is owned by Kape Technologies, a company formerly known as Crossrider, which was at one point associated with adware distribution. Kape rebranded, changed direction, and has since acquired several privacy-focused businesses. The company and CyberGhost both argue that Kape's past has no bearing on current operations. Critics argue that the ownership structure warrants skepticism regardless.

This is a legitimate debate, and we're not going to resolve it for you. What we can say is that CyberGhost itself has not been implicated in any known data breach or logging scandal. There is no documented case of CyberGhost handing over user data that compromised a user's privacy. If Kape's history genuinely concerns you, other independently-owned VPNs may be a better fit. But if you're comfortable relying on CyberGhost's own infrastructure and transparency reports, there's no concrete evidence to suggest the service is compromised.

For additional peace of mind, CyberGhost has undergone independent third-party audits of its no-logs policy (verify the most recent audit details and auditor at vendor site), which is increasingly an industry standard.

## Apps, Ease of Use, and Features

CyberGhost covers all the major platforms: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, Linux (with a command-line interface), and browser extensions for Chrome and Firefox. There are also router setup guides and a dedicated app for some smart TV platforms (verify current supported devices at vendor site).

The desktop and mobile apps are consistently rated among the most beginner-friendly in the industry. The main screen is clean, connection is one click, and the server categories (streaming, torrenting, dedicated IPs) are clearly signposted. You don't need to understand protocols to use it effectively.

For users who want more control, the apps offer protocol switching between OpenVPN, WireGuard, and IKEv2, a kill switch, DNS leak protection, and split tunneling on supported platforms. WireGuard support is a meaningful addition — it offers faster connection speeds and better performance on mobile compared to older protocols.

Torrenting support is solid. CyberGhost labels P2P-optimized servers separately, which simplifies finding servers that permit and perform well for torrent traffic.

## Cons to Consider

No product is perfect, and CyberGhost has some real limitations you should weigh before buying:

1. **Kape Technologies ownership creates genuine trust concerns.** As discussed above, Kape's history with adware is a documented fact. Whether you consider this disqualifying is a personal call, but it's not something a responsible review can gloss over. Users who prioritize ownership transparency may prefer VPNs that are independently run.

2. **No independent audit of the full security infrastructure.** While CyberGhost has published audit results for its no-logs policy, a comprehensive public audit of its entire technical infrastructure and codebase is not available at the time of writing (verify current audit status at vendor site). More rigorous competitors like Mullvad have undergone broader infrastructure audits.

3. **Inconsistent speeds on some server locations.** While major hubs (US, UK, Germany, Netherlands) tend to perform well, more remote or less-populated server locations can deliver noticeably slower speeds. If you specifically need reliable performance in a less common country, do your testing within the money-back guarantee window.

4. **Monthly pricing is expensive.** If you're not willing to commit to a longer plan, CyberGhost's month-to-month cost is among the higher in the market for what you get. Users who need a short-term VPN for a trip or a project may find better short-term value elsewhere.

5. **The browser extensions are limited.** Unlike standalone VPN apps that route all device traffic, the browser extensions only protect traffic within that browser. They're better understood as lightweight proxy tools than full VPN protection.

## Final Verdict

**Rating: 3.8 / 5 stars**

CyberGhost is a genuinely capable, user-friendly VPN that delivers real value — especially for streaming fans and people who want a plug-and-play experience. The dedicated streaming servers are a legitimate differentiator, the app quality is high, the 45-day guarantee gives you a real safety net, and the long-term pricing is competitive.

The reasons it doesn't score higher come down to trust and depth. The Kape Technologies ownership history is a legitimate concern for privacy-conscious users, the audit coverage isn't as comprehensive as some competitors, and the no-logs claims, while backed by a decent transparency report, haven't been stress-tested in adversarial real-world conditions the way some rivals' claims have been.

**Who should buy CyberGhost?**
- Streaming users who want a dead-simple way to access geo-blocked content across multiple platforms
- Beginners who want a clean, intuitive app without a learning curve
- People who are primarily on long-term subscriptions where the price-to-feature ratio is strong
- Households that need to cover multiple devices at once

**Who should look elsewhere?**
- Privacy absolutists who want fully independently-owned VPNs with the most rigorous public audit records
- Users who need only short-term coverage (the monthly pricing is too high)
- Power users who want highly customizable, open-source-friendly tools

CyberGhost is a solid choice for the right user — just go in with an honest understanding of what it is and what questions remain open about its ownership structure.
