## Why iPhone Users Specifically Need a VPN

Apple does a solid job of locking down iOS at the system level, but a locked-down operating system doesn't protect your internet traffic. Your ISP can still see every site you visit. Coffee shop Wi-Fi is still a potential interception point. Apps still leak your IP address. And plenty of streaming services, news sites, and social platforms are still geo-restricted depending on where you are in the world.

VPNs on iPhone also run differently than on desktop. Because of iOS sandboxing rules, VPN apps must use Apple's Network Extension framework, which means they're more tightly controlled than on Android or Windows. That's mostly good for stability, but it also means some advanced features (like custom DNS over system-wide kill switches) can behave differently than their desktop counterparts. The best iOS VPNs work *with* these constraints rather than fighting them.

One more thing worth mentioning: iPhone VPN apps should be judged on how well they handle iOS quirks — things like reconnecting after switching from Wi-Fi to cellular, preserving battery life, and integrating with iOS shortcuts if you want automation. These are the details that separate a good iOS VPN from a great one.

## Cons to Consider

No VPN is perfect, and it's important to go into this with clear expectations. Here are the real limitations you'll run into:

**1. iOS Kill Switch Limitations**
Apple's iOS does not support a true system-wide kill switch the way desktop operating systems do. If your VPN connection drops, there can be a brief window where traffic flows unprotected before the app reconnects. Some VPNs (like ProtonVPN) have engineered workarounds for this using iOS's "always-on" VPN configuration, but it's a platform-level limitation that affects every VPN on iPhone to some degree.

**2. Battery Drain Is Real**
Running a VPN continuously on your iPhone will shorten your battery life. The impact varies by protocol (WireGuard is the most efficient), your signal strength, and how much data you're moving — but expect anywhere from a noticeable to moderate reduction in battery endurance if you leave it always-on.

**3. Speed Reduction on Budget Plans and Congested Servers**
Every VPN slows your connection to some degree. On a fast home connection, you may barely notice. On a slower mobile connection or when connecting to a heavily loaded server, you can see meaningful slowdowns. Free tiers (like ProtonVPN's) are particularly prone to this. Always look for a VPN with a large server network so you have options when one location is congested.

**4. VPNs Don't Make You Anonymous**
This is a widespread misconception. A VPN hides your IP from websites and your traffic from your ISP, but it doesn't make you invisible. Your VPN provider can see your traffic. Logging into Google, Facebook, or any account still links your activity to you. Don't use a VPN as a substitute for general online hygiene.

**5. Some Services Actively Block VPNs**
Streaming platforms like Netflix, Disney+, and others invest in detecting and blocking VPN IP addresses. Even the best VPNs cycle through their server IPs playing a game of cat and mouse with these platforms. Streaming access works — but not always, not from every server, and it can break without warning.
