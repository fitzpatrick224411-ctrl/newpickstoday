## Security and Privacy: How Safe Is Dashlane?

Dashlane uses a zero-knowledge security model, meaning your master password is not supposed to be known to Dashlane or stored in a readable form on its servers. Your vault data is encrypted locally before being synced, so even if Dashlane’s systems were compromised, attackers should not be able to simply read your passwords without your master password or decryption keys.

Dashlane has historically used strong encryption standards such as AES-256, and it supports two-factor authentication for additional account protection. It also supports biometric unlock on compatible devices, which makes the experience more convenient while still reducing reliance on constantly typing your master password.

That said, no password manager can make you invincible. If your device is already infected with malware, if you approve a phishing login, or if you reuse your master password elsewhere, your risk increases. Dashlane gives you tools to reduce risk, but it does not replace good security habits.

Dashlane also supports passkeys, which are increasingly important in 2026. Passkeys can reduce reliance on traditional passwords and make phishing harder when implemented correctly by websites and apps. Dashlane’s ability to store and sync passkeys across devices is useful if you want a password manager that is ready for the gradual move away from passwords.

### Past Incidents and Controversies

Dashlane has not had the same kind of widely publicized vault-compromise controversy associated with LastPass’s 2022 breach, where encrypted vault data was stolen. However, Dashlane has not been controversy-free in the eyes of users.

The biggest criticism has been product direction. Dashlane discontinued its traditional desktop apps and shifted to a web-first experience. Some long-time users disliked losing a native desktop app workflow, especially those who preferred local-feeling software over browser-centric management.

Another point to understand is the VPN. Dashlane’s VPN has historically been powered by a third-party provider rather than built entirely in-house. That does not automatically make it unsafe, but privacy-focused users should review the current VPN provider, logging policy, jurisdiction, and technical details at Dashlane’s site before relying on it for sensitive use.

As always, check Dashlane’s current security whitepapers, audit information, and transparency materials before subscribing if you have strict compliance or privacy requirements.

## Built-In VPN: Useful Bonus or Main Selling Point?

Dashlane’s built-in VPN is the feature that makes the product stand out. Most password managers focus only on credentials, secure notes, passkeys, and sharing. Dashlane adds a VPN to help encrypt your internet traffic on public Wi-Fi and mask your IP address from websites and networks.

For many everyday users, that is genuinely useful. If you often connect to hotel, airport, café, school, or shared office Wi-Fi, having a VPN included in your password manager subscription can make basic privacy protection easier. You do not need to research, buy, and install another product.

However, you should think of Dashlane’s VPN as a convenient privacy add-on, not necessarily a replacement for a top standalone VPN. Dedicated VPN providers often offer more server location choices, advanced protocols, specialty servers, split tunneling across more platforms, streaming optimization, dedicated IP options, multi-hop routing, and more detailed privacy controls.

Dashlane’s VPN may be enough if you want simple one-click protection on public Wi-Fi. It is less compelling if you want advanced VPN features, frequent country switching, router installation, torrent-specific features, or deep control over protocol selection. Also, because Dashlane’s VPN has historically been powered by a third party, careful users should verify the current provider and privacy policy before subscribing.

In short: the VPN adds real value, but it should not be the only reason you buy Dashlane unless your VPN needs are basic.

## Privacy, Dark Web Monitoring, and Identity Features

Dashlane’s dark web monitoring is one of its more useful add-ons. It can alert you if an email address associated with your account appears in known breach data. This does not mean Dashlane can remove your information from the internet, and it does not guarantee that every breach will be detected. But it can be a helpful early warning system.

The password health dashboard is arguably even more important. Many people have dozens or hundreds of old accounts with reused or weak passwords. Dashlane helps prioritize which ones to fix first. Replacing reused passwords with unique, randomly generated ones is one of the biggest practical security improvements most users can make.

Dashlane also supports secure notes and payment information storage, which is helpful for keeping sensitive details out of plain text documents or browser notes. Still, you should be selective about what you store in any password manager. A password manager is designed to protect sensitive information, but centralizing everything in one vault means your master password and account security are extremely important.

For best results, use a long, unique master password, enable two-factor authentication, keep your devices updated, and be cautious about phishing prompts.

## Setup Tips for New Dashlane Users

If you choose Dashlane, spend the first hour setting it up properly. That initial effort matters.

Start by creating a strong master password that you have never used anywhere else. A passphrase made of several random words can be easier to remember than a short complex password. Do not store your master password in your email or notes app.

Next, enable two-factor authentication if available for your plan and setup. Use an authenticator app or hardware security key if supported and practical for you. Save recovery codes somewhere safe.

Then import your existing passwords from your browser or previous password manager. After importing, open Dashlane’s password health dashboard and start replacing reused and weak passwords. Prioritize email, banking, cloud storage, shopping, social media, and work accounts.

Finally, install Dashlane on all devices where you log in regularly. A password manager only works well if it is available at the moment you need it. Set up biometric unlock on your phone and laptop if supported.
