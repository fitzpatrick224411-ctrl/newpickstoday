## Why Budget VPNs Have Gotten Good (And What to Watch Out For)

A few years ago, "cheap VPN" was basically a warning label. It often meant slow speeds, data caps, shady logging practices, or outright malware risk. That's changed significantly. Several reputable, independently audited VPN providers now offer genuinely competitive products at long-term subscription prices that land well under $3/month.

That said, the budget VPN space still has traps. Some providers lure you in with a rock-bottom first-year price and then auto-renew at three or four times the rate. Others are technically free — or nearly free — because they're monetizing your data, which defeats the entire purpose. A few names in this space have been acquired by holding companies with murky ownership histories, which is a real concern.

The five providers we're looking at here have all cleared a basic bar: they've been publicly audited (or are working toward it), they have documented no-logs policies, and their pricing is transparent enough that we can recommend them without feeling like we're sending you into a trap.

## Surfshark: Best All-Rounder Under $3

Surfshark has quietly become one of the most well-rounded budget VPNs on the market. Originally launched in 2018, it was acquired by Nord Security (the company behind NordVPN) in 2022 — a detail worth knowing, since it means two ostensibly competing products now share a parent company. NordVPN and Surfshark have stated they continue to operate independently, but it's a legitimate transparency question you can decide how much weight to give.

**What it does well:** Surfshark allows unlimited simultaneous connections on a single subscription, which is genuinely unusual and makes it excellent value for families or people with lots of devices. It has been independently audited multiple times, covering both its no-logs policy and its infrastructure. The CleanWeb feature blocks ads and malware at the DNS level — it's not a replacement for a dedicated ad blocker, but it adds meaningful protection without extra cost.

Performance-wise, Surfshark uses WireGuard by default on most platforms, which translates to noticeably fast speeds compared to older protocols. Streaming performance is generally solid, with reliable access to major platforms (verify current streaming compatibility at vendor site, as this changes frequently).

**Privacy specifics:** Headquartered in the Netherlands, which is an EU member state and part of the Nine Eyes intelligence-sharing alliance. This is a mild concern for users with very high-stakes privacy needs, though for most everyday users it's not a deal-breaker.

## Private Internet Access (PIA): Best for Power Users and Open-Source Advocates

PIA has one of the longest track records in the budget VPN space, having been in operation since 2010. It's been through legal scrutiny on multiple occasions — most notably when U.S. federal subpoenas were served and PIA was unable to hand over meaningful user data because it genuinely didn't have any. That's real-world proof of a no-logs policy, which is more valuable than most audit certificates.

PIA was acquired by Kape Technologies in 2019. Kape has a controversial history — the company was previously known as Crossrider, which had ties to adware distribution. Kape has since pivoted entirely to cybersecurity products and claims to have cleaned up its act. It's a piece of context you should have; you can decide how much it affects your trust.

**What it does well:** PIA's clients are fully open-source, meaning independent researchers can and do inspect the code. It has a massive server network (35,000+ servers across dozens of countries — verify at vendor site), which helps with both speed and reliability. Customization options are extensive: you can configure encryption levels, choose between different VPN protocols, and fine-tune your connection in ways that budget VPN users rarely get access to.

## Atlas VPN: Cheapest Entry Point, With Caveats

Atlas VPN launched in 2020 and was acquired by Nord Security in 2023, making it a sibling product to both Surfshark and NordVPN under the same holding company. That's a notable consolidation of the budget VPN market under one corporate roof.

At its lowest long-term pricing, Atlas VPN is frequently the cheapest audited-ish VPN on this list. Its SafeSwap feature — which rotates your IP address while you stay connected to the same server — is a genuinely interesting privacy feature you don't see many competitors offering.

**What it does well:** For pure price-per-month value, Atlas VPN is hard to beat. Unlimited connections, decent speeds on WireGuard, and a free tier (with significant limitations) that lets you try before you buy.

**Where it falls short:** Atlas VPN's audit history is less comprehensive than Surfshark's or PIA's. The server network is smaller, which can affect speed consistency in less-covered regions. And the free tier is limited enough in data and server options that it's really more of a demo than a usable long-term product.

## Cons to Consider

No honest review leaves this part out. Here are the real drawbacks you should weigh before buying any of these services:

1. **Long-term pricing lock-in is a common tactic.** Every provider on this list achieves its lowest pricing through 24-month or longer subscriptions. If you cancel or don't renew, the auto-renewal price on shorter plans can be two to four times higher. Read the renewal terms before you hand over your card details.

2. **Corporate consolidation is a legitimate concern.** Surfshark, Atlas VPN, and NordVPN are all owned by Nord Security. PIA and several other VPNs are owned by Kape Technologies. If you care about having genuinely independent providers competing in the market — or you're worried about what happens to your data if ownership changes again — this matters. Mullvad and Proton VPN remain fully independent alternatives, though at higher price points.

3. **A VPN doesn't make you anonymous.** This applies to every VPN on this list without exception. A VPN hides your traffic from your ISP and masks your IP address from sites you visit. It does not protect you from malware you've already installed, browser fingerprinting, cookies, logging into your own accounts, or a determined adversary with legal authority. Budget VPNs marketed with dramatic privacy language are frequently overstating their protection.

4. **Streaming compatibility changes constantly.** Netflix, Disney+, and other platforms actively block VPN IP ranges. A service that reliably unblocks a platform today may not do so in six months. No VPN can guarantee permanent streaming access to any specific service.

5. **Atlas VPN's audit coverage is thinner.** If independent verification of no-logs claims is important to you, Surfshark and PIA are better-documented choices than Atlas VPN at this point in time.
