**2. Fewer simultaneous connections than some rivals.** Surfshark offers unlimited simultaneous connections. ExpressVPN caps you at 8. For most people, 8 is fine — but if you have a large household or run a small business, it's a limitation worth noting.

**3. Owned by Kape Technologies, which has a complicated history.** In 2021, ExpressVPN was acquired by Kape Technologies (formerly Crossrider), a company that previously had a reputation for distributing adware. Additionally, it emerged during the acquisition period that a former ExpressVPN employee had ties to a UAE government surveillance operation — a serious controversy that the company addressed publicly. Kape's umbrella now also includes CyberGhost and Private Internet Access. This doesn't mean ExpressVPN is unsafe to use, but it's a real piece of context that privacy-conscious users should factor in.

No anonymous payment without workarounds.** Unlike Mullvad, which accepts cash and cryptocurrency with no account required, ExpressVPN requires an email address and doesn't make anonymous payment as straightforward. There are partial workarounds (gift cards, Bitcoin), but it's not designed for maximum anonymity.
