## Speed and Performance: Which Is Faster in 2026?

Speed is where NordVPN has historically had the edge, and that remains largely true heading into 2026.

NordVPN's proprietary **NordLynx protocol** — built on WireGuard — consistently delivers some of the fastest throughput in the VPN category. In independent testing across multiple reviewers (verify current details at vendor site), NordVPN shows minimal speed degradation on nearby servers, often retaining 80–90% of baseline connection speeds. On long-distance connections — say, connecting from North America to European servers — performance remains strong. For streaming 4K content, large file transfers, or gaming where latency matters, NordVPN's infrastructure shows.

Surfshark also supports **WireGuard** and has made real improvements in speed over recent years. Its performance is competitive and more than adequate for everyday streaming, browsing, and video calls. However, in direct head-to-head testing, Surfshark typically falls slightly behind NordVPN on raw throughput, particularly on more congested or distant server connections. The gap has narrowed, but NordVPN still holds a modest performance lead.

If you're a heavy user — someone who streams frequently, downloads large files, or works remotely with bandwidth-intensive applications — NordVPN's speed advantage is worth paying for.

## Security Architecture: Protocols, Features, and Audits

Both services offer a solid security foundation, but there are meaningful differences in depth and transparency.

**NordVPN** uses AES-256 encryption on OpenVPN and IKEv2, and its NordLynx/WireGuard implementation uses ChaCha20. It has undergone multiple independent no-logs audits conducted by firms including PricewaterhouseCoopers and Deloitte (verify current details at vendor site). NordVPN also operates a **bug bounty program** and has invested in diskless RAM-only servers under its "Colocated Servers" initiative, which means no data is physically written to disk. Its feature set includes Double VPN (multi-hop routing), Onion over VPN, Threat Protection (a lightweight ad/malware blocker), and a reliable kill switch.

**Surfshark** matches most of these features and adds a few of its own. Its **CleanWeb** feature handles ad and tracker blocking. It offers **MultiHop** (double VPN) connections, NoBorders mode for use in restrictive regions, and a GPS spoofing feature on Android that goes beyond what most competitors offer. Surfshark has also completed independent audits (verify current details at vendor site) and transitioned to a RAM-only server infrastructure.

One distinction: NordVPN has a longer audit track record and its security documentation is more exhaustive. Surfshark is catching up but NordVPN still projects more institutional confidence here.

**Important disclosure:** NordVPN experienced a server breach in 2018 involving a third-party data center in Finland. The breach was disclosed publicly — to NordVPN's credit — and no user data or credentials were compromised due to the no-logs policy. NordVPN has since significantly overhauled its infrastructure practices. This is worth knowing, not as a dealbreaker, but as relevant history.

## Limitations

No honest review leaves this section out. Both products have real weaknesses.

**Surfshark:**
1. **Speed ceiling.** Despite improvements, Surfshark still trails NordVPN in raw throughput benchmarks. Heavy users will notice the difference on bandwidth-intensive tasks.
2. **Shorter audit history.** Surfshark has fewer years of cumulative third-party security audits compared to NordVPN, which may matter to enterprise users or security professionals with high assurance requirements.
3. **Customer support inconsistency.** Surfshark's live chat support has received mixed reviews for response quality, particularly for more technical troubleshooting scenarios (verify current user feedback).

**NordVPN:**
1. **2018 server breach.** As noted above, NordVPN suffered a third-party server compromise in 2018. The company disclosed it publicly and no user logs were exposed, but it happened, and prospective users should know.
2. **Higher cost.** NordVPN costs more, especially on monthly plans. For users who just need a basic, reliable VPN without premium performance requirements, the price premium is hard to justify.
3. **Device limit.** Ten simultaneous connections is generous, but it's still a cap. Large households or users who want to cover every device without thinking about it will find Surfshark's unlimited policy more practical.
4. **Desktop app design.** NordVPN's desktop interface has been criticized by some users for being less intuitive than competitors, particularly when navigating specialty server categories (verify against current app version).

## Final Verdict

**Choose NordVPN if:** You prioritize raw speed, want a longer track record of independent security audits, and are willing to pay a premium for consistently high performance. It's the better pick for power users, remote workers with bandwidth-heavy workflows, and anyone for whom latency or throughput genuinely affects their day-to-day experience.

**Choose Surfshark if:** You need to cover a lot of devices on a single plan without worrying about hitting a connection limit, and you're working with a tighter budget. It's also worth considering if Android features like GPS spoofing are relevant to your use case. Surfshark's security is solid, its speed is more than adequate for casual-to-moderate users, and the value proposition — especially for households — is hard to beat.

**For most everyday users:** Surfshark is the practical winner. The unlimited device policy alone makes it the smarter choice for the majority of households, and the price difference compounds over a multi-year subscription. If you're not running heavy bandwidth workloads, you likely won't notice the speed gap in normal use.

**For performance-first users and security professionals:** NordVPN earns its premium. The infrastructure investment, audit depth, and speed consistency justify the higher cost for users who depend on the VPN for serious work.

Neither product is a guaranteed security solution — a VPN reduces exposure, it doesn't eliminate risk. Always evaluate your specific threat model before making a decision.
